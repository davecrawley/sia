# ADR 0001: Low-observer-effect benchmark contract

- Status: Proposed
- Specification revision: `4479bcc19db72f6ad243a87e4b7271496d60d0b7`
- Requirements: R01, R12, R38, R40
- Baseline artifact: `benchmarks/observer-effect/baseline.yaml`

## Decision

Low observer effect is a property measured by paired experiments, not an architectural assumption. No low-observer-effect claim is permitted while this ADR is proposed, while the baseline contains no completed measurements, or when any required result is failed, missing, invalid, or inconclusive.

The unit of analysis is one independent matched pair of trials. Each pair uses the same workload input, seed, host configuration, power state, frequency policy, concurrency, and initial-state procedure. One trial runs with the observation mechanism disabled and one with it enabled. Apart from the observer mode and the unavoidable work caused by that mode, the arms must be identical.

For endpoint `e`, observer effect is the enabled-minus-disabled paired difference when the endpoint permits zero or has naturally additive units. Strictly positive elapsed time, throughput, and clock endpoints use the log ratio `log(enabled / disabled)`. Ratio margins are directionally symmetric on the log scale. Difference margins are `[-delta, +delta]`. Thus improvements and regressions are treated symmetrically and neither direction may be selectively ignored.

The external benchmark harness is authoritative. Values reported internally by SIA are diagnostic only and cannot establish observer effect.

## Required workloads

All four workloads are required. Inputs and algorithms below are part of the contract and may not be changed after results are inspected. A change creates a new ADR and baseline revision.

| ID | Fixed work and input | Concurrency | Timed boundary | Warm-up |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| `cpu-fixed` | Compute BLAKE3 over an 8 GiB byte stream generated by ChaCha20 with seed `0x5349412d43505501`, in 1 MiB blocks; report bytes processed and final digest | 1 worker | Immediately before generation of byte 0 through validation of the final digest | One complete unmeasured run before every pair |
| `io-fixed` | Generate, write, sync, drop the workload-owned cache through the approved harness facility, read, and BLAKE3-validate a 16 GiB file generated by ChaCha20 with seed `0x5349412d494f0001`; use a dedicated target-filesystem directory | Queue depth 1 | Before the first write through completion of validation and observer teardown | One 1 GiB unmeasured write/read cycle before every pair |
| `accelerator-fixed` | Render 10,000 off-screen 1920x1080 frames of the bundled benchmark scene using seed `0x5349412d47505501`, validate the frame-digest manifest, and explicitly synchronize the accelerator after the final frame | One render queue | Before first submission through final device synchronization and observer teardown | 1,000 unmeasured frames before every pair |
| `gui-active` | Run the fixed 15-minute SIA GUI replay: launch, open overview, CPU, memory, storage, and accelerator views in that order, remain 120 seconds on each view, then repeat from overview until 900 seconds; viewport 1920x1080, scale 100 percent, replay seed `0x5349412d47554901` | One replay driver; normal SIA collector concurrency | Before process launch through final export, flush, teardown, and process exit | One complete five-view navigation cycle before every pair |

The exact executable and input artifact digests, source revision, dependency lock revision, configuration, driver/runtime, and tool versions must be recorded in the baseline before a trial is valid. The fixed algorithm descriptions do not authorize substituting a different corpus, scene, GUI sequence, duration, or concurrency.

The three fixed diagnostic workloads isolate CPU, storage, and accelerator effects. The GUI replay is the required representative workload. Synthetic-only evidence cannot support a target claim.

## Required endpoints and equivalence margins

Each required workload measures all common endpoints. The accelerator endpoints are additionally required for `accelerator-fixed` and `gui-active`.

| Endpoint ID | Definition and trial aggregation | Effect scale | Equivalence margin | Practical rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `target.elapsed_seconds` | External monotonic wall-clock duration, seconds; one value per trial | Log ratio | `[-log(1.03), +log(1.03)]` | A change of 3 percent is the largest tolerable user-visible duration effect |
| `target.throughput_per_second` | Validated completed work divided by authoritative elapsed time; one value per trial | Log ratio | `[-log(1.03), +log(1.03)]` | A 3 percent sustained-rate change is operationally material |
| `sia.cpu_seconds_per_minute` | SIA user plus system CPU seconds over the complete boundary, normalized to one minute | Difference | `[-0.30, +0.30]` seconds/minute | More than 0.5 percent of one CPU is material on the minimum target |
| `sia.peak_rss_mib` | Maximum externally sampled SIA resident set, MiB | Difference | `[-64, +64]` MiB | 64 MiB is the memory budget reserved for observational overhead |
| `sia.io_read_mib_per_minute` | External process-accounting read bytes normalized to one minute | Difference | `[-2, +2]` MiB/minute | Larger background reads can perturb storage-sensitive work |
| `sia.io_write_mib_per_minute` | External process-accounting written bytes normalized to one minute, including deferred export | Difference | `[-2, +2]` MiB/minute | Larger writes can perturb storage and endurance |
| `target.gpu_clock_mhz` | Time-weighted mean target-engine clock over the boundary | Log ratio | `[-log(1.03), +log(1.03)]` | A 3 percent clock displacement can indicate contention or throttling |
| `target.gpu_power_watts` | Time-weighted mean board or package accelerator power | Difference | `[-2, +2]` W | Two watts is material to the target power and thermal envelope |
| `target.gpu_temperature_celsius` | Maximum accelerator temperature during the boundary | Difference | `[-2, +2]` degrees C | Two degrees can alter boost or throttling behavior |

For a log-ratio endpoint the reported estimate and interval are exponentiated for presentation, but equivalence is decided on the log scale. The corresponding ratio bounds are approximately `[0.970874, 1.03]`, not `[0.97, 1.03]`.

Trial distributions are preserved as arm-labeled raw observations. Means are used for paired effects after the declared transform; peak RSS and peak temperature remain trial maxima before pairing. No percentile may be substituted after inspection.

## Measurement-quality gates

Every attempted trial records expected and received sample counts, sample-loss count and rate, collector-lateness maximum and p99, and the applicable limits. Every `gui-active` trial also records externally measured SIA GPU busy time and GPU activity percent.

A trial is invalid when sample loss exceeds 0.5 percent, p99 collector lateness exceeds 1.5 sampling periods, maximum lateness exceeds 3 sampling periods, timestamps move backwards, or required quality fields are absent. A `gui-active` trial is also invalid if SIA GPU activity is absent. A workload result is inconclusive if invalidation or loss leaves fewer than the fixed required pair count. Measurement quality is never imputed and is not converted into a favorable performance result.

## Confidence and equivalence decision

Let `M` be the number of required workload-endpoint conclusions materialized by the baseline applicability matrix. Under this ADR, `M = 30`: 24 common conclusions plus six accelerator conclusions.

Each effect receives a two-sided Student t confidence interval with family-wise Bonferroni coverage. The per-effect two-sided error is `0.05 / M`, so each interval has confidence `1 - 0.05 / M`. This provides at least 95 percent simultaneous family-wise coverage without relying on endpoint independence. The resampling or variance unit is the independent pair; individual samples inside a trial are never treated as independent replicates.

For each endpoint, calculate the paired transformed values, their mean, standard deviation, standard error, and the interval using `t_(1 - 0.05/(2M), n-1)`. When a predeclared order or period term is required, use the treatment coefficient and its t interval from the fixed model described below, with the same adjusted critical probability.

Equivalence is established only if the complete adjusted confidence interval lies strictly inside both predeclared margin bounds. Touching or crossing a bound is not equivalence. Margin overlap, failure to reject a conventional difference test, an unadjusted interval, or a favorable point estimate is explicitly insufficient.

An overall result is `pass` only when all 30 conclusions pass. Any failed conclusion makes the overall result `fail`. Any missing, invalid, or inconclusive conclusion makes it `inconclusive` unless another conclusion has already failed, in which case the overall result remains `fail` while all conflicts are still reported. Results may not be selectively summarized by workload, endpoint, direction, or hardware.

## Pairing, order, and environment control

Pairs use randomized AB/BA order, where A is disabled and B is enabled. A recorded 256-bit experiment seed initializes a counter-based generator. Within each workload, successive pairs are allocated in permuted blocks containing equal numbers of AB and BA orders. The generated schedule is committed to the baseline before measurements begin.

The disabled arm runs the same SIA build, launch path, GUI state where applicable, external harness, samplers, output destinations, and duration. Only the observation mechanism under evaluation is disabled. Enabled-arm buffering, serialization, export, flush, synchronization, child processes, and teardown are inside the measurement boundary. Deferred work may not be moved outside that arm.

Run one pair at a time on an otherwise isolated target. Disable unrelated scheduled work, networking not required by the workload, automatic updates, screen blanking, and dynamic power-profile changes. Record CPU affinity, NUMA placement, governor, turbo setting, accelerator power limit and clock policy. Pair arms use identical settings.

Before each arm, restore the workload input, output directory, application configuration, cache policy, and thermal starting window. Start only when CPU and accelerator temperature are within 2 degrees C of the pair baseline and no throttling reason is active. Apply the stated workload warm-up, then a 120-second idle stabilization. Storage cache dropping is allowed only through the same recorded privileged harness facility for both arms.

The harness records order, period, pre-run cache state, pre/post temperatures, throttle flags, background CPU, disk and accelerator utilization, and warm-up results. A trial is objectively invalid if an undeclared process consumes more than 2 percent of one CPU for over 10 seconds, unrelated storage traffic exceeds 64 MiB, a throttle flag appears, the starting thermal window is missed, validation fails, or the declared cache state cannot be established.

Carryover is checked before outcomes are unblinded by comparing each arm's recorded pre-run cache state, temperatures, throttling flags, and background-activity gates. Any mismatch invalidates the pair. The 120-second stabilization is increased to 300 seconds for the whole experiment if any of the first 12 pilot pairs fails the thermal return window; this choice is made from pre-arm state only and is recorded before confirmatory runs.

Confirmatory analysis uses the fixed crossover model `effect ~ observer_mode + period + order` for every endpoint. The observer-mode coefficient is the effect of interest. The period and order coefficients are always retained, whether statistically significant or not. Workload is not pooled: each workload-endpoint cell is analyzed separately. Cache state and target-hardware stratum must be constant; if multiple approved hardware strata are intentionally studied, each is reported separately and every claimed stratum must pass.

## Repetition and precision

The confidence target is the simultaneous 95 percent family-wise procedure above. The numerical precision target is an adjusted confidence-interval half-width no larger than one half of the applicable equivalence half-margin.

For each workload, first collect 12 independent pilot pairs using the final protocol. Pilot observations determine variance only and never enter confirmatory estimates. For endpoint `e`, let `s_e` be the pilot standard deviation of paired transformed effects and `h_e` half of its equivalence half-margin. Calculate

`n_e = ceil((z_(1 - 0.05/(2M)) * s_e / h_e)^2)`.

The fixed confirmatory pair count for a workload is the maximum `n_e` over its applicable endpoints, rounded up to an even number and bounded to at least 30 and at most 200. Commit all calculated values and the resulting count before confirmatory collection. If the calculated count exceeds 200, collect no confirmatory evidence for a claim and mark the workload inconclusive pending a revised ADR. If a fixed run loses pairs, replacements follow the existing hidden schedule until the fixed count of valid pairs is reached or 240 attempted confirmatory pairs is reached. Reaching that maximum without the required valid count is inconclusive.

There is no interim equivalence testing, sample-size re-estimation, or early stopping. Consequently optional stopping cannot inflate type-I error. Repeating the experiment after inspecting confirmatory results requires a new preregistered baseline revision; results from repeated attempts must remain disclosed.

## Failed, missing, censored, and anomalous runs

Every attempted pilot and confirmatory trial receives a monotonically increasing attempt ID and remains in the baseline. Record start/end timestamps, arm, pair, period, order, exit status, timeout state, validation result, inclusion state, and one predeclared reason code.

Predeclared exclusion reasons are: `harness-failure`, `target-failure`, `timeout`, `validation-failure`, `quality-gate`, `thermal-gate`, `throttling`, `background-interference`, `cache-state`, `power-state`, `carryover-gate`, and `operator-abort-before-outcome`. Timeouts use the fixed limit of twice the pilot median elapsed time or 30 minutes, whichever is smaller; the limit is committed before confirmatory collection. A timeout is not replaced by a censored favorable value. Paired analysis excludes the whole pair if either arm is excluded. Partial endpoint records remain preserved.

Exclusion decisions use harness state, quality fields, validation, and exit status, and are made before arm outcomes are compared. Statistical outliers are not excluded. No trimming, winsorization, imputation, post-hoc censoring, or outcome-dependent rerun is allowed.

## Harness calibration and asynchronous work

Before pilot collection, measure the external harness alone for 30 repetitions at the configured sample rate and record its CPU time, RSS, I/O, timer overhead, sample loss, and lateness. Calibration passes only if the upper adjusted 95 percent bound for timing overhead is below 10 percent of the smallest applicable equivalence margin and quality gates pass. Failure makes the experiment inconclusive until the harness or sampling rate is changed under a new baseline revision.

Use one monotonic host clock for boundaries and timestamps. Record wall-clock synchronization metadata when multiple devices or processes contribute events. Accelerator completion is defined by an explicit device synchronization, not command submission. Buffered samples, observer export, device synchronization, child-process shutdown, and observer teardown complete before the enabled timer stops.

## Provenance and claim scope

The baseline records source and ADR revisions, configuration digest, observer mode, workload and input identity, experiment seed, host and target identifiers, OS, kernel, firmware, CPU, memory, storage, accelerator model, driver and runtime, power and frequency settings, environment controls, and every measurement-tool version.

Development-host results are labeled `development` and may guide engineering only. Target claims require measurements on the declared target hardware and software image. An unmatched host, synthetic-only workload set, incomplete endpoint coverage, absent raw observations, uncalibrated estimates, or missing provenance cannot support a target low-observer-effect claim.

## Consequences

This procedure is deliberately capable of returning failure or inconclusive. Passing requires complete evidence from the declared target and every simultaneous interval inside its margin. The proposed ADR and empty baseline establish only the measurement contract; they do not establish low observer effect.
